It started with a Google Form.

One minute we were zoning out in class, the next we were signing up for the **ICTAK Techathlon 2024-25**. The theme? **NextGenAI**. The location? Infopark, Koratty. The vibe? Pure chaos with a side of ambition.

## About the Team Name

"Team Rizzler" was not planned. While filling in the registration form between classes, we used it as a placeholder and forgot to replace it.

Flashback to the initial roll call:

    "And next up... Team Rizzler?"

The organizers paused and double-checked the list. We sheepishly raised our hands as the room burst into laughter.

A meme was born before the hackathon had even started.

## What We Built

You know that feeling when you're staring at course options wondering which one won't destroy your future? Yeah, that's what we wanted to fix.

We built an **AI-powered FYUGP Course Pathway Recommender** — it takes your psychometric profile, Holland career codes, and academic records, runs them through an AI/ML prediction model, and spits back your best-fit course track plus the skill gaps standing between you and it.

> "We read your mind so your academic counselor doesn't have to."

The stack was a Python backend (trained on Google Colab), NLP for processing what students actually mean, a React frontend, and Azure for deployment. Mathew Geejo, Shayen Thomas, and I built it all in under 24 hours, which is either impressive or concerning.

## How the Recommendation Flow Worked

The prototype combined three kinds of information instead of pretending one quiz could decide a student's future:

1. A psychometric profile described preferences and working style.
2. Holland career codes grouped those interests into familiar career-oriented categories.
3. Academic records added evidence about the subjects in which the student was already performing well.

The Python backend prepared those inputs for the recommendation model. The result was not just a course name: the interface returned a suggested FYUGP pathway and highlighted skill gaps the student could work on next. A React frontend kept that flow understandable during a very short judging demo, while Azure hosted the pieces we needed online.

It was a hackathon prototype, not an academic counselling replacement. The useful part was showing how several signals could support a conversation instead of reducing a student to one score.

## The Tech Stack

We built this with:

- **Python backend** (Google Colab for training and fine-tuning our AI models)
- **NLP** for processing what students actually mean
- **Basic frontend** (React, because we like to suffer)
- **Azure for deployment** (using student credits)

## The Victory Moment

When they called us the winners and then struggled to pronounce "Rizzler" with a straight face, we experienced that rare moment when imposter syndrome takes a break.

The judges later told us they loved our project but were equally entertained by our team name presentation. Apparently, our awkward explanation of "it was an accident" added charm points.
